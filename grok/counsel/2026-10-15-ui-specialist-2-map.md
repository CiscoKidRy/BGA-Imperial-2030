# Map layout, 2026-10-15

Map area only of `grok/ui-design-2026-10-15.md`. The plan was not edited. Checked against empty board 574713, 1024 by 718. This is a placement note for an original schematic. It is not a trace of the publisher illustration, and that illustration is not copied into a file.

The schematic is one column. The scoring track is the top band. The map is the middle. The six nation trays are the bottom band. Track and trays stay pinned. Only the map scrolls or zooms. No north-south wrap.

The track is cells 0 through 25 across the full width. Cell 0 is separate and has no factor on it. The factor text is x0 over 1-5, x1 over 6-10, x2 over 11-15, x3 over 16-20, x4 over 21-24, and x5 over 25 only. Six octagons start on 0, lettered R, C, I, B, U, and E.

## Land, sea, and the fixed pieces

Use flat fills, not the printed painting. Home fills, with the name in text on the land: United States red in North America; Brazil green in South America; Europe dark blue, darker than the sea, in the northwest of the old world; Russia purple across the north from Murmansk to Vladivostok; China yellow, yellower than the neutral tan; India charcoal, with light text, south of China. Each home has the four cities named in the plan, and no fifth city.

Cities, as placed on this frame, without drawing a border that is not visible: San Francisco west, Chicago inland, New York east, New Orleans south. Manaus northwest, Fortaleza northeast, Brasilia inland, Rio de Janeiro southeast. London on the northwest island, Paris west, Berlin east of Paris, Rome south. Murmansk northwest, Moscow west, Novosibirsk center, Vladivostok on the far east coast. Urumqi west, Beijing north, Chongqing center, Shanghai on the east coast. The umlauts in Urumqi are not readable on this frame; the schematic uses the plan's later reading, Ürümqi. New Delhi north, Mumbai west, Kolkata east, Chennai south.

Neutral land is one tan, labeled wherever a name is readable. Names read on this frame: Alaska, Canada, and Quebec north of the United States; Mexico south of it; Colombia, Peru, and Argentina around Brazil; Ukraine between Europe and Moscow; Turkey south of that; Kazakhstan and Mongolia between Russia and China; Afghanistan, Iran, and the Near East west of India; North Africa, Guinea, Nigeria, East Africa, Congo, and South Africa; Korea, Japan, Indochina, the Philippines, Indonesia, Australia, and New Zealand. Do not add a land name that is not in that list. Do not invent the line where two regions meet. An unlabeled island east of South Africa, and an unlabeled blue island in the north ocean, are not regions.

Unlabeled white land lies along the top, north of the North Atlantic and west of Murmansk. It has no piece target and no name. Do not label it Greenland.

Seas are light blue and named in text. North Pacific is the upper left ocean, west of North America. South Pacific is below a visible split line, west of South America. North Atlantic is between the Americas and Europe. Caribbean Sea is north of Brazil. South Atlantic is east of Brazil. Mediterranean Sea is between Europe and North Africa. Gulf of Guinea is west of Africa. Indian Ocean is east of Africa and west of Australia. China Sea is east of the Philippines. Sea of Japan is east of Japan. Tasman Sea is between Australia and New Zealand. That is the plan's eleven. Do not add a sea, and do not turn the Pacific split line, or any other printed line, into a fleet border. Panama's west mouth is still unsettled on this frame. Do not draw it. Suez is not given a new coast here.

Switzerland is a small white field with a red cross on Europe, between Paris and Rome. The word Switzerland is required. It is not a home province and takes no army.

The rondel sits in the South Pacific, west of South America, south of the Pacific split, and above the Russia tray. Under the hub, the text is "1 to 3 fields for free". The eight wedge names are the plan's clockwise order, in text. This frame shows Factory, Taxation, Maneuver, and Production on the upper half. The other four names are not re-read here. The picture is not the small-screen control.

The tax chart sits in the sea south of Africa, clear of the land, clear of the rondel, and short of Australia. Its right side reaches the Indian Ocean. It keeps the plan's three row labels and column headers, including 18+. A highlighted column is not enough. The three values are also text.

The trays are one row, equal widths, in this order: Russia, China, India, Brazil, USA, Europe. Each prints that name. USA's accessible name is United States. The turn pawn sits on the active tray. Color alone does not identify a tray.

## Pieces

A standing army is an upright figure in the nation color, with the word hostile when it stands in another home. A lying army is the same figure on its side, with the word friendly. A fleet is a ship in side view, in the nation color, with the word fleet. A brown factory is a brown square, not nation-colored, with the word armament. A light-blue factory is a light-blue circle, a different shape from the square, with the word harbor, so it is not read as sea. This note does not assign square or circle to a city. The 1024 frame is too soft for that, as the plan says. A flag is a small flag on a pole, in the nation color, with the nation name. A stack is one icon plus a numeral. Players do not count overlapping sprites. Hostile rail is a gray stroke inside that one home province, with the words rail closed. The land fill is not grayed. Open rail stays a non-gray stroke and stops at the home border. Do not invent which cities the stroke joins. Rome-Turkey and Paris-London are the only extra crossings named here, and only as labels. Broken orange strokes are not given endpoints and are not borders.

## Text, zoom, and the wrap arrows

Text, not color alone, is required on every home, city, neutral name listed above, sea, Switzerland, factory type, army pose, fleet, flag, stack count, rail state, track number, factor, octagon, tax header, tray, rondel wedge, and wrap arrow.

The five codes are paired by the same latitude on this frame. They name the sea on the other edge. Left side, top to bottom: SJ in the upper North Pacific, below Alaska; CS beside the words North Pacific; CS in the upper South Pacific, left of those words; TS left of the rondel. Right side, top to bottom: NP in the Sea of Japan; NP in the China Sea; SP on the island side of that sea; SP above New Zealand, on the Tasman side. The four links are SJ with the Japan NP, the North Pacific CS with the China Sea NP, the South Pacific CS with the upper SP, and TS with the lower SP. Each control shows the code and the full sea name: North Pacific, Sea of Japan, China Sea, South Pacific, Tasman Sea.

At 1280 pixels wide, the default scale fits the whole map, both arrow edges, the rondel, and the tax chart between the track and the trays. Track cells can be at least 44 pixels. Tray names fit. City and wedge targets cannot all be 44 pixels at that fit, so a play zoom enlarges until the active target is at least 44 by 44, and the map pans. Whole-map, plus, and minus are 44-pixel buttons. When a rondel wedge would be under 44 pixels, the wheel opens beside the map instead of over a sea.

At 390 pixels wide, the world does not fit. The default scale shows one nation group or one ocean, and every legal target is at least 44 by 44. The track stays on top and scrolls sideways, one cell at least 44 pixels, rather than shrinking. The trays scroll sideways in the same order, each name still readable, each tray at least 44 pixels tall. The South Pacific wheel is not tappable. The eight space names are a list, each row at least 44 pixels. Tax columns scroll sideways at 44 pixels, and a 44-pixel control returns to the chart under Africa. Pinch may enlarge. It may not shrink a legal target below 44 pixels.

The wrap arrows stay on screen while the map scrolls. They are not only ink at the far edges. At 1280 they are 44-pixel targets on the two map edges. At 390 they are four 44-pixel chips under the track: NP-SJ, NP-CS, SP-CS, and SP-TS. Choosing one pans to that sea and keeps the other sea's name on the chip. The map does not wrap north to south.
