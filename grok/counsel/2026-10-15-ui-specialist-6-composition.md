This review covers grok/ui-design-2026-10-15.md and does not change that plan. The primary action is one filled button in the sticky top guidance banner, naming whose decision it is and the single legal choice, and that button takes focus first.
At 1280px the page is the banner, then the 0-25 scoring track, then one row: the map, tax chart, and six trays stack in the main column, while the investor panels and the log stay in a side column.
At 390px the same regions stack in one column: banner, six score chips, map, rondel list, tax chart, six trays, investor panels, then log. The side column moves under the trays.
The page never scrolls sideways. The map pans and zooms only inside its frame. If the track or tax chart needs extra room, that scroll stays inside the track or the chart.
Every banner button, legal city, rondel space, canal answer, bond, tray header, and score marker is at least 44 by 44px, with space between targets. The glyph may be smaller than the hit area.
The keyboard ring is :focus-visible: a 2px #FFFFFF gap and a 2px #1A1A1A ring. It stays visible on white, on China yellow, on India black, and on Europe blue. Tab order starts at the banner choice.
Do not use emoji. Armies, fleets, factories, and flags are drawn shapes plus a letter. A ship, flag, or building emoji is not an icon.
Nation color stays on the swatch and the land, not on the words. Every name and banner label is #1A1A1A on #FFFFFF, which is 17.4:1. Yellow text on white is about 1.07:1 and fails. Black text on white is 21:1, so India black passes contrast, but it matches ordinary text and is not a nation cue.
The second encoding is the initial R, C, I, B, U, or E, plus a pattern: Russia diagonal, China dots, India solid, Brazil horizontal, United States vertical, Europe a cross. Brazil green and United States red are never the only difference.
Chip letters meet 4.5:1: white on Russia #5E2B8A (9.6:1), India #1A1A1A (17.4:1), Brazil #146C2E (6.5:1), United States #B42318 (6.6:1), and Europe #1D4E89 (8.4:1). China is #1A1A1A on #F5C400 (10.6:1), with a #1A1A1A border, because that yellow on white is only 1.6:1.
Pieces on matching land get a contrasting stroke, white on India and black on China. The plan's dark unaffordable rondel spaces and bonds are not enough. The banner says they are short, or leaves them out. A dimmed control is never the primary action.
These plan passages will hide the next decision if they become dialogs: the setup panel, the factory, production, and import prompts, the canal yes-or-no, hostile or friendly, the battle demand, the flag question, the bond prompt, taxation shown before they commit, and the small-screen rondel if it opens over the map.
Keep each of those in the banner, with the map, chart, and trays still visible. Name the canal, the maneuver step, or the Swiss Bank force in the banner and highlight that spot on the map. A tooltip cannot be the only city readout at 390px, where there is no hover.
During tax, the banner shows that column's bonus and power, and the chart repeats it underneath. It does not cover the chart. Setup and the experienced bond choice sit on the page above the trays, not in a modal, so prices and treasuries stay in view.
The eye lands on that one banner button. The track, map, tax chart, six trays, side panels, and log are context. A legal city or rondel space may mirror the banner, but it is not a second primary.
